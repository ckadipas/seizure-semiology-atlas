How to use this page

Find a sign or paper

Search for a symptom, brain area or paper. Open Filters to narrow the results by timing, side, classification or study details. The filters apply to all three tabs. Clear filters starts over.

Explore the brain

Choose DKT40 Atlas to select colored brain regions, or Brodmann Labels to select numbered areas. Drag to turn the brain and pinch or scroll to zoom. The view buttons and Left / Right change what you see; use Laterality to filter the side reported in a study.

Select and clear areas

Tap an area on the map, or search and check names in the adjacent menu. Click outside the menu to close it. You can combine areas to find reported findings linked to each selection. Selections stay active when you switch atlases. Clear selection removes all map selections; Reset view only resets the view.

Read the results

Signs groups the linked signs. Weighted evidence summarizes study support for a brain area or side. Sources brings results from each paper together. Associated study findings contains other linked records, such as case descriptions or comparisons. Use Organize signs by to change the grouping.

Open a paper’s findings

Open Results beneath a paper’s title for findings, reported measurements and source details. View all results from this paper shows the rest of that paper’s results. DOI links open the article.

Understand counts and scores

Show counts adds the number of matching results to Brodmann labels. It is available in Brodmann mode. Dots indicate one, two, or three or more papers. A score combines study weights; it is not a probability. About the scores explains the calculation.

About the scores

Each study contributes once for a sign and for localization or lateralization. Its weight combines the recorded evidence class, study method, and sample size. The score adds these study weights.

Dots show the number of papers: one, two, or three or more. They do not indicate certainty. Scores are not probabilities or pooled study results.

When a filter selects only part of a study’s results, that study’s score is withheld. Reviews and cited reports remain available without counting the same study again.
